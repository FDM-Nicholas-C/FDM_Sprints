Show how a complaint is handled without the proposed assistant.

• Cover intake, investigation, evidence search, recommendation, approval, response and closure.

• Record systems, handoffs, delays and rework.

A bank receives customer complaints through phone, branch, web and email channels. Each complaint may involve different products, events, documents and customer interactions. To investigate it, an analyst often needs to combine the customer’s narrative with structured records such as account details, complaint history and service requests, then find the relevant policy or procedure and decide what should happen next. This information is usually spread across systems and documents, which can make handling slow, inconsistent and difficult to audit. 

Intake: 

- Customer complaint received

- Systems: phone, branch, web, email

Investigation:

- Customer narrative

- Customer record

- Customer issue

- Customer complaint history

- Company policy

Evidence Search:

- Search structured records (account details, complaint history, service requests)

- Find relevant policy or procedure

Recommendation:

- Follow company policy

- Approval

- Deny

- Escalate

- Handoff: Escalation means the complaint is handed off to the Supervisor

Approval:

- Customers claim is accepted as true

Response:

- Customer’s complaint is satisfied

Closure:

- Customer complaint ticket is closed
